    Buying a Japanese knife

      Where to buy

      March hotel deals

      The big money moves
      • Go early March if you can. Prices stay fairly quiet until about March 20, then climb into cherry-blossom season (forecast peak around Mar 28–Apr 5; no official 2027 forecast until about late Dec).
      • Osaka is the value base. Its March 3-star average (~$104/night) is about 40% under Kyoto (~$175). Kyoto is 15–30 min away by train, so 2 nights in Kyoto plus day trips from Osaka saves real money.
      • Book an apartment-style room for 4. One quad room with a kitchen beats two doubles on price and on fun.
      • Book refundable now, then re-check monthly. Rebook if the price drops.
      • Kyoto lodging tax is now per person per night: ¥400 for rooms ¥6,000–19,999 per person, ¥1,000 for ¥20,000–49,999. Small for four friends sharing a mid-range room.

      Good fits for 4 friends

      • MIMARU (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka): apartment rooms built for groups of 4, with kitchens and laundry. The default pick.
      • Design hostels with private quad rooms: the cheapest way to get a great location. Filter for "private room, 4 guests".
      • A ryokan night (Hakone or Kyoto): one splurge with tatami, yukata, dinner and onsen. Many charge per person.
      • Neighborhoods: Tokyo: Shinjuku or Shibuya for nightlife, Ueno or Asakusa for value. Kyoto: near Shijo/Kawaramachi. Osaka: Namba or Shinsaibashi.

      Before-you-go checklist

        Japan basics

        • Money: cards work most places now, but small shops, shrines and food stalls want cash. Carry ¥10,000–20,000 and use 7-Eleven ATMs.
        • Transit: add a Suica or PASMO card to Apple Wallet or Google Wallet and tap everywhere, even at konbini.
        • Shinkansen: for a Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka trip, single tickets usually beat the JR Pass since its 2023 price increase. Do the math with your real route.
        • Luggage forwarding (takkyubin): send big bags hotel to hotel for about ¥2,500 each and ride the train light. Ask your front desk the day before.
        • Data: buy an eSIM before you fly (Ubigi, Airalo, or your carrier's plan).
        • Power: same plugs as the US (2-prong) and 100V. 3-prong plugs need a cheap adapter.
        • Arrival: fill out Visit Japan Web for customs before landing to get the QR codes.
        • Manners: no eating while walking in busy areas, quiet on trains, no tipping, carry a small trash bag (public bins are rare).
        • Konbini: 7-Eleven egg sandwiches, Lawson fried chicken, FamilyMart melon pan. A cheap breakfast every day.
        • Departure tax: it rose to ¥3,000 in July 2026. It's built into airfare.
